  How it works, tips & limitations
  
Formats

  
Clean text joins captions into readable paragraphs (breaking at pauses and sentences). Timestamps gives one [m:ss] line per moment so you can cite exact parts. SRT is the standard subtitle file — drop it into a video player or editor.

  
Languages

  
The picker lists every caption track the video actually has (manual uploads and auto-generated ones, marked auto-generated). Your last picked language is remembered for later videos and playlist batches.

  
Playlists

  
Paste a playlist link (or any video link with &list=…) to load all its videos, then select which ones to fetch. Combined output uses whichever format chip is active. Batches are capped at the first 100 videos and run one-by-one to stay polite to the relays.

  
How it reaches YouTube

  
First it asks YouTube's own internal API directly from your browser — no relay, and on normal home networks YouTube answers happily (this is what saves videos when the relays are blocked). Caption files are then fetched through Piped's open media proxy, which forwards them with CORS enabled. If any of that fails, it falls back to the Piped API, Invidious instances, and generic CORS proxies, trying each source in order until one answers. Nothing is installed or sent anywhere else.

  
Limitations

  
Private, age-restricted, members-only and captionless videos have no accessible transcripts. YouTube also "bot-walls" datacenter IPs: the public relays sometimes get challenged for individual videos ("Sign in to confirm you're not a bot") — the direct-from-browser path usually rescues those; if both fail, wait a minute and retry. Auto-generated captions have no punctuation, so "clean text" for them is one long stream of words broken only by pauses.






  Runs entirely in your browser — no install, no keys, no tracking; history lives in your browser's localStorage.
